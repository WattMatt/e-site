## Primitive 1 — work items, assignment and ball-in-court

### 1.1 How ownership is modelled today

Every module invented its own answer to "who owes the next move", and four of the seven invented nothing at all.

| Entity | Owner column | Status vocabulary | Due date | Who closes | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| RFI | `assigned_to` nullable → `public.profiles` | `draft, open, responded, closed` | `due_date` nullable, never defaulted | anyone in the org | `00002_projects_schema.sql:79-98` |
| Snag | `assigned_to` nullable → `public.profiles` | `open, in_progress, resolved, pending_sign_off, signed_off, closed` | none | `signed_off_by` | `00004_field_schema.sql:10-31` |
| QC entry | **none** | `conformance` (`pass\|fail\|na`, default `na`) + `severity` (`minor\|major\|critical`, nullable) — a conformance verdict, not a lifecycle | none | report-level `status` only | `00172_qc_reports.sql:110-125`, `00176_qc_conformance_and_hardening.sql:53-71` |
| Inspection | `assigned_to_id` + `verifier_id` → `auth.users` | `assigned, in_progress, awaiting_verification, certified, re-inspect_required, abandoned` | `scheduled_at` only | `verifier_id` | `00066_inspections_module.sql:54-77` |
| Site form | **none** (`created_by`/`submitted_by` imply it) | `draft, submitted, distributed, void` | none | `distributed_by` | `00179_site_forms.sql:69-105` |
| Diary delay | **none** | **none** | none | nobody | `00002_projects_schema.sql:146-158`, `00017_diary_entry_types.sql:14-18` |
| Order line | **none** | `by_tenant, required, ordered, received` | `ordered_at`/`received_at` are records, not targets | nobody | `00083_node_orders.sql:50-86` |

QC deserves the correction it gets above: `00176` gave every entry a verdict and a severity, so a QC report now carries machine-readable defects. What it did not give an entry is an **owner** or a **per-entry lifecycle state** — `qc_entries` still has `sort_order`, `created_by` and nothing else that says who is fixing this or whether it is fixed (`00172:110-125`).

Three defects explain the production numbers directly.

**RFIs cannot be assigned through the UI.** `apps/web/src/app/(admin)/rfis/new/page.tsx:156-157` carries the comment *"No assignee field — RFIs are team-wide"*, so `assigned_to` is only ever populated by the project default at `packages/shared/src/services/rfi.service.ts:79-82`, reading `projects.project_settings.default_rfi_assignee_id` (`00101_project_settings.sql:29`). No project has that set, so all 15 live RFIs are unassigned. `apps/web/src/actions/rfi.actions.ts:70` already instruments this (`assignee_source: 'none'`) — the diagnostic shipped and the answer was never acted on.

**Due dates are fetched and thrown away.** `projectSettingsService.getRfiDefaults` returns `dueDays` (`packages/shared/src/services/project-settings.service.ts:325-340`), `default_rfi_due_days` defaults to 7 (`00101_project_settings.sql:30`) — and `rfiService.create` writes `due_date: input.dueDate || null` (`rfi.service.ts:98`), ignoring it entirely.

**Closing is ungated.** `closeRfiAction` (`apps/web/src/actions/rfi.actions.ts:182-234`) checks authentication and nothing else — no `requireEffectiveRole`, unlike `diary.actions.ts:124-127`. The only backstop is `00027_rls_insert_policies.sql:47-50`, which permits UPDATE to any active member of the owning org, on any project, member or not. `00161_client_viewer_readonly_write_block.sql:64,96-101` blocks `client_viewer` writes on `projects.rfis` by RESTRICTIVE policy; every other role, contractor included, passes. Any contractor can close any RFI in WM's org.

There is no "assigned to me" surface anywhere: the only query filtering on an owner column in the whole monorepo is `packages/shared/src/services/snag.service.ts:39`, and the dashboard has none.

### 1.2 Decision: a mirrored spine, not a migration

**Decision: create `projects.work_items` as a spine that mirrors source entities; do not migrate entities wholesale and do not add assignment columns in place.** Wholesale migration would rewrite seven modules and every PDF renderer that reads them in one quarter. Columns-in-place gives no single queryable inbox — My Work would be a seven-way UNION across four schemas (`projects`, `field`, `structure`, `inspections`) with four RLS models. The mirror is the only option where Q1 ships an inbox without touching the modules that currently work.

**Division of authority.** Source tables remain the system of record for domain content and their own status. The work item owns assignment, gatekeeper, due date, ball-in-court and the universal status. Source status is pushed one-way into the mirror by a per-source `AFTER INSERT OR UPDATE` trigger; assignment flows the other way by write-back trigger for the two sources that have a usable owner column (`rfis.assigned_to`, `snags.assigned_to`), so existing readers keep working. `inspections.inspections.assigned_to_id` is read at mirror time as the seed assignee but is deliberately **not** written back — the inspection engine's own assignment flow (`00066`) stays the system of record for that column, and a third write-back is a third loop to reason about.

**Schema: `projects`.** Already exposed to PostgREST, so no `db_schema` PATCH and no `PGRST002` risk.

**The DDL is Appendix A(a)**, which reproduces this section's column set verbatim and is the block every other section cites; it is not repeated here.

**Decision: typed nullable FKs, one per source, not a polymorphic `(schema, table, id)` triple.** A polymorphic key cannot be enforced, and an orphaned row in a personal inbox — an item you cannot open — is the worst failure this primitive can have. Seven columns is the cost; an eighth mirrored type costs one column.

**Decision: `<= 1` sources, not exactly one.** A hard `= 1` permanently forecloses the ad-hoc item — "send me the updated single-line by Friday" has no source entity in any module, and that sentence is exactly what today lives in a WhatsApp group. `work_items_source_required` keeps full referential discipline for every mirrored type while leaving the sourceless ones legal. It is declared as **`item_type IN ('task','approval') OR status = 'void' OR <exactly one source column is non-null>`**, and both sourceless types are real rather than headroom: `task` is registered in Q1 and `approval` in Q3 (Appendix A(b)). Naming `approval` in the constraint now is deliberate — relaxing this later would mean an `ALTER` on the hottest table on the platform, and Q3 should cost a registry row, not a constraint rewrite.

**Decision: `ON DELETE SET NULL`, never `CASCADE`.** The house precedent states the reason: `field.site_forms.node_id` is set-null because *"deleting a board must not delete the record of having made it safe"* (`00179_site_forms.sql:75-77`). Deleting a diary entry is a live, gated action today (`deleteDiaryEntryAction`, `apps/web/src/actions/diary.actions.ts:109-150`), so a cascade would silently delete the work item and, through it, its `work_item_events` — destroying the median-days-to-respond metric whenever an RFI is deleted. Instead, a **`BEFORE DELETE`** trigger on each source sets the item `status = 'void'`, `void_reason = 'source deleted'`. The item leaves every inbox (BIC is null on void, which `work_items_bic_present` permits) and the events survive. **`AFTER DELETE` does not work and is worse than it looks:** the `ON DELETE SET NULL` referential action runs first, so an `AFTER` trigger's `UPDATE` matches nothing — and `work_items_source_required` is re-evaluated on that `SET NULL` while the item is still `open` with no source, so the **DELETE aborts with `23514`** and deleting an RFI, snag or diary entry stops working entirely (derived from A(a)'s constraint set and measured 2026-09-10 against the real table). The same constraint is why a **closed** item is voided too rather than left closed with a null FK: that is forced by `work_items_source_required`, not chosen. The trigger's WHERE carries `origin = 'mirror'` so A(a)'s partial source indexes are usable and a manual task that merely links a source is left alone.

**`ref` generation.** Only `projects.rfis` carries a number today (`rfi_number INTEGER GENERATED ALWAYS AS IDENTITY`, `00002:83`) and that identity is global, not per-project — "RFI-12" would not be the twelfth RFI on the project. `ref` is `<TYPE>-<n>` where `n` is a per-project, per-type counter allocated by a `BEFORE INSERT` trigger reading `MAX(...) + 1`, exactly the `qc_reports_ensure_no` pattern (`00172:91-105`), with `UNIQUE (project_id, ref)` above it. The ref numbers **work items, not source rows**: RFI refs are renumbered per project at backfill and no longer track `rfi_number`, which stays displayed alongside as the module's own identifier.

**Indexes are the single set in Appendix A(a)** and are not restated. One property of that set belongs to this section because §1.3 depends on it: the per-source unique index is partial, predicated `WHERE <col> IS NOT NULL AND origin = 'mirror'`. The mirror trigger only ever writes and updates `origin = 'mirror'` rows, so a `split` or `manual` row sharing the same source FK is legal and is never touched by source-status pushback.

**Trigger loop suppression is normative for all six mirrored sources.** Every mirror and write-back trigger is declared as **two triggers per source** — `AFTER INSERT … FOR EACH ROW` and `AFTER UPDATE OF <cols> … FOR EACH ROW WHEN (OLD.<col> IS DISTINCT FROM NEW.<col> OR …)` on the specific columns it cares about. A single declaration is not valid PostgreSQL: `ERROR: 42P17: INSERT trigger's WHEN condition cannot reference OLD values`. Every trigger function returns immediately when `pg_trigger_depth() > 1`; and every write-back writes only when the target value actually differs. Without all three, the write-back into `projects.rfis.assigned_to` fires `rfis_updated_at` (`00002:100-102`) and the mirror trigger, which updates `work_items`, which fires the write-back again. This is the single most likely way the primitive takes production down and it is not left to the implementer.

**Measured, correcting an earlier claim that removing "both guards" produces `54001`:** with the `WHEN` predicates in place, **any ONE of the three defences ends the cycle** — removing **all three** (the wrapper's depth guard, the write-back's value predicate, and the `_upd` triggers' `WHEN` clauses) is what produces `54001 stack depth limit exceeded`. The depth guard is kept as *the* termination guard because it alone does not depend on values converging: the value predicate stops amplification and the `WHEN` stops the trigger firing at all, but only the depth guard is unconditional.

**The load-bearing `SECURITY DEFINER` is the WRAPPER, and the layering is grants → definer chain → RLS.** On the shipped path a contractor's `INSERT INTO projects.rfis` never reaches `work_items_insert_gate`: `mirror_rfi_work_item()` is SECURITY DEFINER, so the whole projection runs as postgres, which simultaneously satisfies section G's EXECUTE revokes and makes `SET row_security TO 'off'` effective — strip the definer and the EXECUTE revoke answers first (`42501` on `project_rfi`); only after also granting EXECUTE does item 2's RESTRICTIVE spine gate become the thing that refuses. ⚠ That three-layer mutant is therefore a **counterfactual**, not evidence that RLS protects the shipped path — it grants exactly what section G revokes. The unmutated proof that the spine gate bites a real client session is probe 16's arm 3, `contractor_cannot_write_that_row_by_hand`. `project_rfi()`'s own `SECURITY DEFINER` is pure defence-in-depth: its only callers are the definer wrapper and the backfill, both already running as postgres.

**Trigger privileges.** The mirror and write-back functions are `SECURITY DEFINER SET search_path TO 'projects, public' SET row_security TO 'off'`, with `REVOKE ALL ... FROM PUBLIC` **and** an explicit `REVOKE ... FROM anon`, verified with `has_function_privilege('anon', oid, 'EXECUTE')` — the `00171:112-113` and `00107:35-38,76` pattern. They must be definer because they write `assignee_id`, `gatekeeper_id` and `due_date`, values the contractor who raised the RFI must not be able to forge, and because otherwise the new RESTRICTIVE INSERT policy on `work_items` (§1.9) would be evaluated against that contractor and their perfectly legitimate RFI insert would fail. Attribution inside them uses `auth.uid()`, never `current_user`, which resolves to the function owner.

**The direct-INSERT rule, stated once.** Client `INSERT` into `work_items` is permitted for the **two sourceless types** — `task` in Q1, `approval` in Q3 — and for **`order_followup`**, whose only entry point is the explicit chase control on an order line, because `structure.node_orders` carries no projection trigger (A(b)) and nothing else can create that item. All three are gated by `projects.user_can_write_work_item(project_id, item_type)` in the app **and** by a RESTRICTIVE INSERT policy in the DB, because a server action is directly invocable and page-level gating is not a gate; the `order_followup` arm of that policy admits an insert only when it names `node_order_id` and nothing else. **Every mirrored type stays source-only**: the source row is the only entry point, a direct insert naming one of the six mirrored keys is refused by the same policy, and for those types `user_can_write_work_item` gates only reassignment, due-date change, void and close.

`projects.work_item_events` (append-only: created, assigned, reassigned, status changed, due changed, closed, voided) supplies the median-days-to-respond metric and the audit trail for a manual ball-in-court shift.

### 1.3 Single assignee

**Decision: exactly one assignee, enforced by `assignee_id uuid NOT NULL` — the constraint is the schema.** There is no join table to make co-assignment possible, so it cannot be reintroduced by a UI change. This follows Linear and Asana's published rationale (research 03, Asana: with two assignees one person may think the other has the ball), and it is why co-assignment is rejected outright rather than offered.

Work that genuinely needs two people is split into two items sharing a source row: the second is inserted with `origin = 'split'`, which the per-source unique index deliberately excludes, so a deliberate split is possible while an accidental double-mirror is not. A split row carries its own `ref`, its own assignee and gatekeeper, and its own lifecycle; the source's status no longer drives it.

In the UI, assignee is a required single-select on every create form, pre-filled with the resolved default (§1.5) so it is never a blank field the user must think about. Reassignment is one control on the item, never a multi-select.

### 1.4 Ball-in-court is generated, not typed

`ball_in_court_id` is a `STORED` generated column over `status`, `assignee_id` and `gatekeeper_id`. It cannot drift, cannot be forged by a direct PostgREST write, and cannot be left null while the item is open — the `work_items_bic_present` CHECK documents that intent and protects it against a later edit of the expression.

| Type | `open` → BIC | `answered` → BIC | Trigger for `answered` |
|---|---|---|---|
| RFI | assignee | gatekeeper | a row lands in `projects.rfi_responses` |
| Snag | assignee | gatekeeper | source status reaches `resolved` or `pending_sign_off` |
| QC defect | assignee | gatekeeper | a close-out photo on the entry, or a `projects.qc_comments` row by the assignee |
| Inspection | assignee | gatekeeper (`verifier_id`) | source status reaches `awaiting_verification` |
| Diary action | assignee | gatekeeper | assignee marks done |
| Form action | assignee | gatekeeper | the form's submit gate clears |
| Order follow-up | assignee | gatekeeper | assignee marks the chase answered on the item — `structure.node_orders` carries **no projection trigger** (A(b)), so an order line reaching `ordered`/`received` never moves the item on its own |
| Task | assignee | gatekeeper (the creator) | assignee marks done |

**It is derived from workflow state rather than typed because a typed field is a second thing to remember and therefore a field that goes stale** — the same reason `assigned_to` is null on 15 of 15 RFIs.

The one exception is Procore's manual shift, and it must be specified precisely because the naïve version does not work. `ball_in_court_id` is generated from whichever of the two person columns the current status selects, so **the shift writes that column**: `assignee_id` while `triage` or `open`, `gatekeeper_id` while `answered`. A PM handing an answered RFI to a different reviewer changes the gatekeeper; writing `assignee_id` in that state would regenerate an unchanged BIC and look like a broken control. This ships as one server action, `reassignWorkItem(item_id, user_id)`, which selects the column from the current status, plus a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger that rejects a write to the column the current status does not select. Both column changes are recorded in `work_item_events`. Only an `ORG_WRITE_ROLES` user or the current BIC holder may shift. The shift emits `work_item_reassigned`, and `ball_in_court_changed` when the holder actually changes; Appendix A(c) marks both `always_fires`, so the notification primitive delivers them regardless of category preference or project mute.

### 1.5 Due dates and defaults

**Decision: the per-type settings live in `projects.project_settings` as `work_item_defaults jsonb NOT NULL DEFAULT '{}'`, keyed by `item_type`, with keys validated against `projects.work_item_types` by trigger** (a CHECK cannot reference another table). Typed columns were rejected: eight types in Q1, each new one forcing a migration on a hot 1:1 table carrying an audit trigger (`00102_project_settings_history.sql:30,61-63`). `default_rfi_due_days` (`00101:30`) seeds the `rfi` key and is thereafter read-only for backwards compatibility.

Each key holds `{ days_to_respond, triage_owner_id, gatekeeper_id }`. **The platform defaults — the per-type due offset, the calendar each type uses, the default assignee when the creator names nobody and the gatekeeper rule — are Appendix A(b)** and are not restated here. Two of those defaults carry a rationale that belongs to this section rather than to the registry:

- **`diary_action` is the shortest offset on the board** because a delay recorded today is stale by Friday; a delay item that surfaces a week late is a record, not an action.
- **`form_action` feeds a supplementary CoC under EIR reg 7(4)**, so its offset is set by what the record is for, not by how long the form takes to fill.

**Snag gatekeeper is the project PM, not the raiser.** Nothing in the schema or the code ties sign-off to the raiser: `field.snags.signed_off_by` is a bare nullable FK (`00004:25`) and `signOffSnagAction` (`apps/web/src/actions/snag.actions.ts:59-95`) stamps whoever clicked, with no role gate beyond authentication and a close-out-photo check. In production, snags are raised on site and signed off by WM, so a raiser-closes default would hand close authority to the contractor who reported the defect — the opposite of the manager model §1.8 adopts. The raiser is auto-added as a watcher instead. Raiser-closes for internal snags is a per-project settings key if WM ever wants it, not a platform default.

**"Project PM" is resolved by one rule, in the same order as `00107`:** the oldest active `projects.project_members` row with role `project_manager`; else the oldest active org member with role `project_manager`; else org `admin`; else org `owner`. Org-level owner/admin/PM auto-win on every project without any `project_members` row (`00107:59-61`), so a project with no explicit PM row still resolves.

**Stale settings must never block a source write.** Storing `triage_owner_id`/`gatekeeper_id` in jsonb means no foreign key, and `public.profiles` cascades from `auth.users` (`00001:62`) — so a departed employee's id survives in the jsonb and a naïve mirror insert against `assignee_id NOT NULL REFERENCES public.profiles(id)` would raise, aborting the transaction and making it impossible for anyone to raise an RFI on that project. Two defences: the jsonb-validation trigger verifies each referenced id still resolves to an active profile with an effective role on the project and nulls stale keys on read; and the mirror trigger walks the full resolution chain and terminates at the org owner, never raising.

**Decision: the work-item due date is computed on Appendix A(h)'s calendar, and the JBCC engine is not touched.** A South African working-day calendar already exists and is contractually live: `addWorkingDays` / `computeDeadline` / `crossesBuildersHoliday` at `packages/shared/src/lib/jbcc/working-days.ts:25,37,67`, used by the JBCC time-bar engine at `apps/web/src/actions/jbcc.actions.ts:295`. It hardcodes Mon–Fri plus SA public holidays and ignores `project_settings.working_days`, `extra_holidays` and `builders_holiday` (`00101:20-23`) entirely — that is the real gap, not the absence of a calendar. A(h) closes it by materialising the one statutory source into `projects.public_holidays` and exposing `projects.working_days_between(...)` plus its TypeScript mirror, with two named calendars: **`office`** (`working_days` as stored, minus holidays) and **`site`** (`office` plus Saturday). Work-item due dates read those, per type, from A(b)'s `calendar` column.

⚠ **The JBCC engine keeps a fixed statutory calendar and never reads `project_settings`,** because JBCC defines "working day" in the contract, not in a project preference, and `deadline_date` is stored. A test asserts a JBCC deadline is byte-identical after `working_days` is edited. `lib/jbcc/working-days.ts` becomes a shim that passes the statutory calendar explicitly. **An earlier draft of this section instructed that "JBCC is switched onto the extended helper in the same PR"; that instruction is deleted** — it is exactly what the byte-identity test forbids, and a project preference must never be able to move a contractual time-bar.

The `BEFORE INSERT` trigger on `work_items` performs the working-day computation against A(h)'s calendar, with a calendar-day fallback (`now()::date + days_to_respond`) for rows created by a path that passed no date at all — mobile, direct PostgREST — so `due_date NOT NULL` always holds. An unseeded calendar year raises rather than silently drifting by a day, which is A(h)'s rule and the reason `calendar_years` exists. Aconex's mandatory "response required" is the precedent (research 02): an item without a target date cannot exist.

### 1.6 Triage

Unowned inbound never floats, and the guarantee lives in the right place.

**Decision: `projects.project_settings.triage_owner_id uuid REFERENCES public.profiles(id)` is NULLABLE.** A `NOT NULL` column with no default would break project creation platform-wide: `projects.ensure_project_settings_row()` is an `AFTER INSERT` trigger on `projects.projects` that inserts only `(project_id, organisation_id)` (`00103_project_settings_backfill_and_autocreate.sql:19-30`), so a third mandatory column makes that insert raise and abort the transaction that created the project. The same migration updates `ensure_project_settings_row()` to resolve a default — the project's `created_by`, else the org owner — so new projects arrive with a named triage owner, and backfills the 14 existing rows the same way. **The DB-level guarantee is `work_items.assignee_id NOT NULL`, not a settings column.**

The mirror trigger's resolution chain is: explicit assignee → per-type `work_item_defaults.<type>.triage_owner_id` → project `triage_owner_id` → the §1.5 PM resolver → the org owner.

**The status an item is born in follows from that chain, not from a rule of thumb.** An item created **with** an explicit assignee is inserted `status = 'open'`. An item created **without** one is inserted `status = 'triage'` with `assignee_id` = the resolved triage owner. Ball-in-court is therefore non-null from the first millisecond of the row's life either way, and the Triage queue only ever contains genuinely unowned inbound — which is what makes the Q1 metric "inbox items read" measurable rather than diluted by items already routed to a named person.

The Triage queue is a filter over `work_items`, not a separate table: `project_id = ? AND status = 'triage'`. Its only actions are assign (the transition `triage → open`), set due date, or void. Contractor-raised RFIs — 11 of 15 today — land here rather than in nobody's hands.

### 1.7 Q1 types and the registry

`projects.work_item_types` is the registry. **Its columns, the type list per quarter, and each type's source, default due offset, calendar, default assignee and gatekeeper rule are Appendix A(b)** and are not restated here.

**Q1 registers eight types:** `rfi`, `snag`, `qc_defect`, `inspection`, `diary_action`, `form_action`, `order_followup` and `task`. Six are mirrored from a source table; `order_followup` has a source table but no projection trigger (A(b)) — it is created only by the explicit chase control on an order line. `task` is sourceless and is registered in Q1, not held back as schema headroom — it is the row that gives a monday.com line ("send me the updated single-line by Friday") somewhere to land, and without it Q1 ships an inbox that can only reflect work some other module already created. `instruction` follows in Q2, `approval` in Q3 as the second sourceless type, and `valuation` in Q4.

A new **mirrored** type costs: one registry row, one nullable FK column, one arm on both source CHECKs, one mirror trigger mapping that module's status onto the five universal states, one scoped unique index, and one row in `docs/rbac-matrix.md`. That is a single reviewable migration plus a mapping function — roughly a day, versus the two-to-three weeks each existing module spent inventing its own lifecycle. A new **sourceless** type costs one registry row and nothing else, which is why `approval` in Q3 requires no `ALTER` on `work_items`: `work_items_source_required` already names it (§1.2).

### 1.8 Status, gatekeeper and distribution

Five universal states (`triage, open, answered, closed, void`) with the source's own vocabulary mirrored into a display-only `source_status`. An unmapped source status leaves the universal status unchanged and is recorded in `source_status` only. Three distinct roles, per Procore's RFI Manager model (research 02):

- **Assignee** — exactly one, owes the next move while `triage` or `open`.
- **Gatekeeper** — exactly one, the only person who may set `closed`. Enforced by a `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger, not by RLS, because RLS cannot compare OLD and NEW. The house already has this exact pattern: `projects.qc_reports_status_guard` (`00172_qc_reports.sql:249-267`), a `BEFORE UPDATE` status guard written for the same reason and documented at `00172:34-40`. Copy it, including the rule that made the site-forms transition trigger inert on first ship: **the guard compares `auth.uid()` to `NEW.gatekeeper_id`; `current_user` resolves to the function owner in a `SECURITY DEFINER` context and must never be used for authorisation.** This is what stops the current behaviour where any org member closes any RFI.
- **Distribution** — `projects.work_item_watchers (work_item_id, user_id, reason)`, many rows, notification-only, never blocking. Auto-populated on create, assign and @mention (Linear's subscription model).

Watchers replace two fan-outs, not one. `createRfiAction` sends a bell to every active project member on every RFI (`apps/web/src/actions/rfi.actions.ts:84-95`, `resolveProjectRecipients`) and an email to the same roster (`:98-105`); the diary path does the same through `notifyDiaryEntryAction` → `notifyDiaryEntryCreated` (`apps/web/src/actions/diary.actions.ts:72-97`). Together they produce the 964 notifications of which **750 are `diary_created`** and 57 have ever been read. This section replaces the RFI half with BIC-plus-watchers; the notification primitive owns the diary half and the digest that makes both survivable.

### 1.9 RLS and role gates

**SELECT is permissive and deliberately wider than the write gate:**

```sql
CREATE POLICY work_items_select ON projects.work_items FOR SELECT TO authenticated
USING (
  public.user_has_project_access(project_id)
  OR assignee_id    = auth.uid()
  OR gatekeeper_id  = auth.uid()
  OR EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM projects.work_item_watchers w
             WHERE w.work_item_id = projects.work_items.id AND w.user_id = auth.uid())
);
```

Gating on `user_effective_project_role(project_id) IS NOT NULL` would have been narrower than the source tables and than the assignment rule, producing the worst failure an inbox can have — a user who sees the RFI in the module list but not its work item. `projects.rfis` SELECT is org-wide for non-client-viewers (`00034_client_viewer_project_scope.sql:103-115`) **or** `user_has_project_access` (`00160_cross_org_project_visibility.sql:62-66`), while `user_effective_project_role` returns NULL for an org contractor with no `project_members` row (`00107:59-66`) — and `00107`'s own header says the function *"does NOT gate ACCESS"* (`00107:19-21`). The three identity clauses guarantee that whatever is in your inbox, you can open.

**An item can never be assigned to someone who cannot open it.** `assignee_id` is an FK to `public.profiles` with no membership predicate, so any profile in the database is nominally assignable. A `BEFORE INSERT OR UPDATE` trigger asserts `public.user_effective_project_role(project_id, NEW.assignee_id) IS NOT NULL`, and the same for `gatekeeper_id`; the assign UI's people-picker reads the same set.

**Writes** are RESTRICTIVE INSERT/UPDATE/DELETE policies in the `00171` pattern (`00171_markup_write_roles_rls.sql:115-146`) calling a new `projects.user_can_write_work_item(p_project_id uuid, p_item_type text)`, which resolves the type's `write_roles` from the registry. That function is `SECURITY DEFINER SET search_path TO 'public' SET row_security TO 'off'`, takes `auth.uid()` rather than `current_user`, and `COALESCE`s the role comparison so a non-member yields FALSE rather than NULL. It needs `REVOKE ALL FROM PUBLIC` **and** an explicit `REVOKE ... FROM anon`, verified with `has_function_privilege('anon', oid, 'EXECUTE')` — never by reading `proacl`.

**Grants.** `projects` is covered by `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES IN SCHEMA projects GRANT SELECT ON TABLES TO anon` (`00025_grant_schema_permissions.sql:20,26`), and unlike `cable_schedule` and `structure` that default was never revoked (`00168_cable_schedule_security_hardening.sql:92-98` revoked only those two). All four new tables would therefore be born with a standing `anon` SELECT grant, leaving RLS as the only thing between an unauthenticated PostgREST caller and the inbox — the posture `00168` was written to end. The spine migration adds `REVOKE SELECT ON projects.work_items, projects.work_item_events, projects.work_item_watchers, projects.work_item_types FROM anon`, verified after apply with `has_table_privilege('anon','projects.work_items','SELECT')` and never by reading `relacl`. The durable one-line fix, `ALTER DEFAULT PRIVILEGES IN SCHEMA projects REVOKE SELECT ON TABLES FROM anon`, goes in the same migration.

**Client viewers may be assignees from Q1; their write carve-out lands in Q3.** The approved frame weights clients and developers equally with WM and contractors, and in a shopping-centre fit-out the landlord frequently *is* the ball-in-court — a decision on a tenant variation. A blanket `00161`-style block therefore cannot be the permanent answer: it means a client can never move an item to `answered`, so any item pointed at them sits open forever while `work_items_bic_present` guarantees it keeps pointing at them, and their inbox can never reach zero. **Decision: a `client_viewer` may be an assignee from Q1, and the write set that lets them clear an item is §11.1's Watcher tier row — read, comment and approve on the surfaces §11.5 lists — delivered in Q3 alongside the portal consolidation, not in Q1.** The write set is enumerated once, there, and never here.

Until it lands, `00161`'s blanket block stands unchanged and an item assigned to a client viewer is moved to `answered` by its gatekeeper on their behalf, recorded in `work_item_events` like any other transition. When it lands, the mechanism is a RESTRICTIVE UPDATE policy whose `WITH CHECK` admits `client_viewer` only when `assignee_id = auth.uid()`, with the row difference confined to `status → 'answered'` by the same `BEFORE UPDATE` trigger that guards the gatekeeper; `00161`'s block continues to stand for every other column and every other table.

Two further carve-outs, both in Q1: the current `assignee_id` may always UPDATE their own item (that is how a contractor answers), and `work_item_events` is INSERT-only to `authenticated` with no UPDATE or DELETE policy at all. Because server actions and `app/api/*` routes sit outside `(admin)/layout.tsx` and are directly invocable, every mutation additionally calls `requireEffectiveRole`; the DB policies are the backstop, not the gate. `docs/rbac-matrix.md` and `CONFORMANCE.md` are updated in the same PRs.
